We can't measure the pull directly. So what can we measure?

In Investigation 2 you found that the pull on a valence electron is stronger with more protons and weaker with more distance. Scientists can't put a force meter inside an atom. They measure two other things instead.

Atomic radius

Atomic radius tells us the size of an atom: the typical distance from the nucleus to its outermost (valence) electrons. Atoms don't have a hard edge, so this is an average. It is measured in picometers (pm). One picometer is a trillionth of a meter.

First ionization energy

First ionization energy is the energy that must be added to remove one valence electron from a single, separate atom. It is measured in kilojoules per mole (kJ/mol).
